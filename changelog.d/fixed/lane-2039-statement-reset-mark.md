- **[inference]** A method call on `self` between a nil guard on an instance variable and its read no longer brings back a false `possible nil receiver` report. ([#2469](https://github.com/rigortype/rigor/pull/2469))
  - Applies when Rigor cannot prove which instance variables the called method writes and the method's body shows no write of `nil` to the variable, such as a call to an `attr_reader` or to `respond_to?` in `return unless @tag; items; t = @tag; t.upcase`, or to a method that rewrites it as `@tag = @tag.strip`.
  - A called method whose body assigns the variable `nil`, a safe-navigation call or a conditional that may give `nil`, as `def clear_tag = (@tag = nil)` does, still reports, including one that runs other code Rigor cannot follow.
  - So does one that assigns `a || nil`, `a && nil`, the second target of `@a, @tag = 1` or of a nested `(@a, @tag), @b = [1, nil], 2`, while a `case ... in` with no `else`, which raises rather than giving `nil`, no longer reports. ([#2585](https://github.com/rigortype/rigor/pull/2585))
  - A method whose `nil` comes from a computed value (`@tag = lookup(key)`), from `define_method`, `attr_writer`, `instance_variable_set`, `send` or `method_missing`, or from a method more than 8 calls deep no longer reports.
